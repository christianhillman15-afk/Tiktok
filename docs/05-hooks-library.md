# Hooks Library (50+)

The first line is the whole ballgame. On TikTok the algorithm decides in the first 3 seconds whether your teardown lives or dies, and a skeptical plumber scrolling in another state decides in the same window whether you're talking about *him*. This file is a swipe file: 50+ ready-to-say opening lines for the "I build websites that book more jobs for home-service and trades businesses — anywhere in the US" creator, sorted into eight hook types. Steal them, swap in your target trade, and pair them with the content pillars and CTAs from the strategy — never sell the build in the hook, sell the free teardown. Launch Media builds the site that books the jobs, then runs the marketing that keeps the phone ringing; your reach is national, but every client you land is still a local business, so keep "near me," "your town," and "when someone Googles you" wherever it's about *their* customers.

> **How to use this:** Write the hook *before* the video. Say it out loud in line one, front-load your target search phrase, and burn it into the on-screen caption word-for-word. Every hook below is written in owner-language — calls, booked jobs, "looking legit," "Googled at 9pm" — never web jargon. Keep 70-80% of your posts educational/entertaining; only 20-30% touch the offer.

---

## The First-3-Seconds Rulebook

Open on the payload, not on your face and never on "hey guys." Within the first three seconds you must do four things at once: (1) **say the target search phrase out loud** in line one and burn it into the caption — the exact words an owner would type ("plumber website," "small business website," "why isn't my business on Google"); (2) **name the trade out loud and on-screen** so the right person feels called out and everyone else scrolls (that's fine — you want *people like me*, and a plumber in Ohio and a roofer in Texas should both stop cold); (3) **make one specific, concrete promise or claim** — a number, a flaw count, a before/after tease — not a vague "let's talk about websites"; and (4) **show a visual that can't be ignored in frame one** — a cursor on a broken mobile site, an empty Google result, a split-screen glow-up — because motion + a real screen beats a talking head for the cold-start swipe test. Cut hard, no intro, no logo sting, no throat-clearing. If the first sentence could be sentence three, delete it. You're aiming for ~70% completion, so the hook has to create a loop the viewer *has* to see close — and it has to double as an evergreen search headline that still pulls views six months from now.

## 1. Callout / Negative Hooks

**When it works:** Top-of-funnel reach on teardown videos. A negative or accusatory frame stops the scroll because it triggers "wait, is that *me*?" — the owner self-identifies in the comments, which surfaces warm leads and feeds the algorithm. Never name or show an identifiable business; blur it, or roast your own old work. Validate their hustle later in the video — the hook is allowed to sting, the video is not allowed to insult their success.

1. "Your website is losing you jobs and you don't even know it."
2. "If your plumbing site looks like this on a phone, you're handing calls to your competitor."
3. "This is why nobody's calling — and it's not your prices."
4. "Your business card looks better than your website. That's a problem."
5. "3 things on this roofer's site are quietly costing him customers."
6. "Stop blaming the economy — your website hasn't been updated since 2017."
7. "You paid your nephew $200 for this website, and it shows."
8. "Nobody bounces off your reviews. They bounce off your homepage — in 50 milliseconds."

## 2. Curiosity Gap Hooks

**When it works:** Opens a loop the viewer has to close, which is the single biggest lever on completion rate (your ~70% target). Best on teardowns and myth-busting. The trick: tease the payoff without naming it, then deliver it before the video ends. Don't fake the loop — if you promise "the #1 thing," the #1 thing had better be worth the wait.

9. "There's one thing on almost every trades website that makes owners lose money — and it's above the fold."
10. "I Googled your business so you don't have to. Here's what your customers actually see."
11. "The reason your phone isn't ringing has nothing to do with your work."
12. "Most pest control pros have no idea their site is invisible at 9pm. Here's the test."
13. "There's a 30-second check that tells you if your website is costing you jobs. Watch."
14. "Your competitor isn't better than you. There's one reason Google shows them first."
15. "I found the exact spot where this HVAC site loses every customer. Timestamp 0:07."
16. "Nine out of ten trades sites I open are missing the same free thing — and it's not what you think."

## 3. Before / After Reveal Hooks

**When it works:** Mid-funnel desire. Before/afters are the most save- and share-worthy format you have because the owner instantly imagines their own glow-up. The hook's job is to promise a satisfying payoff and, ideally, attach a business result ("5x the calls"), not just "it looks nicer." Split-screen or time-lapse; the number in the overlay is what converts.

17. "Same shop. New site. From 2 calls a week to booked out — watch the flip."
18. "I gave this painter a new website in 3 hours. Before vs after."
19. "$500 website vs $5,000 website — same business, side by side. Guess which one gets the calls."
20. "Watch me turn this dead Facebook-only trades business into a real website in 60 seconds."
21. "This is what your site looks like now... and this is what it could look like Monday."
22. "From 'is this business even open?' to 'book me now' — same electrician, new site."
23. "He was embarrassed to hand out his URL. Here's the before and the after."
24. "One weekend rebuild. Before: nothing on Google. After: showing up first for 'near me.'"

## 4. Number / List Hooks

**When it works:** Lists promise structure and a clear finish line, which lifts completion and makes the video easy to script and skim. Great for evergreen search assets — "3 things," "5 mistakes," "the one fix." Keep the number small (3 beats 7) so the payoff feels achievable and the video stays tight.

25. "3 things killing this landscaper's website — number 2 is on almost every site I open."
26. "5 mistakes I see on every trades website. You probably have at least 3."
27. "The 1 thing your website needs above the fold — and 90% of home-service businesses skip it."
28. "4 seconds. That's how long you have before someone decides your business looks legit or dead."
29. "3 free fixes that'll make your site get more calls this week — no rebuild required."
30. "2 numbers every owner should know: 75% judge you by your website, 76% call within 24 hours."
31. "The 3-page website that books more jobs than a 12-page one nobody reads."
32. "5 signs your website is scaring off customers instead of booking them."

## 5. Trade Callout / Us-vs-Them Hooks

**When it works:** Directly attacks the "not relevant to my industry" belief — the real #1 objection. Calling out the specific trade out loud makes "people like me" feel safe and specific, and an us-vs-them framing (you vs the competitor down the road who outranks you) taps the strongest emotional buying trigger. Call out the *trade*, not a city — you're reaching owners nationwide — but you can still lean on "your town" and "near me," because their customers are local even when your market isn't.

33. "I build websites for home-service and trades businesses anywhere in the US — and most of them are invisible on Google."
34. "Your competitor down the road isn't busier because he's better. He just shows up first."
35. "Every plumber in your town is fighting over the same 'near me' searches. Here's who's winning and why."
36. "If you're a contractor without a website, someone else in your town is getting your calls tonight."
37. "I roasted 10 roofing sites this week. Only one didn't need me."
38. "The trades businesses booking out solid all did the same thing to their website. Here it is."
39. "Two electricians, same skills. One's slammed, one's slow. The difference is on their homepage."
40. "Comment your trade and I'll tell you the #1 reason businesses like yours aren't getting found."

## 6. Objection-Flip Hooks

**When it works:** Trust stage and pure search gold — owners type these exact phrases ("I have a Facebook page," "word of mouth," "websites are too expensive"). The move is to say the objection *in their words*, then flip it. Never attack their success — validate word-of-mouth and hustle first, then show the gap. These are your evergreen education pillar.

41. "'I get all my business from word of mouth.' Love it — but here's where word of mouth dies: Google."
42. "'I have a Facebook page.' That's a house you built on rented land. Let me show you why."
43. "'A website's too expensive.' What's one new customer worth to you? Because it only has to bring you one."
44. "'I don't have time for a website.' That's exactly why you shouldn't be building it — watch."
45. "'I tried Wix myself.' You didn't fail. You spent 40 hours on something that still doesn't get found."
46. "'People already know me.' The friend they refer you to Googles your name first. What shows up?"
47. "'My work speaks for itself.' It does — right up until someone can't find you at 9pm."
48. "'I don't need a website in my trade.' 97% of people search online for what you do. Let's do the math."

## 7. Result / Proof Hooks

**When it works:** Bottom-of-funnel proof that closes fence-sitters — the pinned video and the DM follow-up. Lead with the business outcome (calls, jobs, revenue) in the first two words, not the aesthetics. Real screen-recorded proof — a client text, Google call-clicks before/after — beats any claim. This is what converts the people who already follow you.

49. "From 2 to 11 leads a month. Here's exactly what we changed on this roofer's site."
50. "His text after launch week: 'the phone won't stop ringing.' Here's the site that did it."
51. "This cleaner's call-clicks went up 5x in 30 days. Same business, new website."
52. "We rebuilt this electrician's site for $199 a month. Look what happened to his bookings."
53. "Booked out in 3 weeks. This is the before, this is the after, this is the number."
54. "Google says this site got 340 'call' taps last month. A year ago it didn't exist."
55. "Watch me build a $199/mo site for a plumber — and the result it got 60 days later."

## 8. Pattern-Interrupt Hooks

**When it works:** Anywhere you need to smash the scroll — a physical action, a weird claim, a hard visual cut, or an unexpected sound in frame one. Best when the swipe-away rate is high (broad, cold audiences). Use sparingly and always pay it off with substance, or you train viewers not to trust you.

56. "Stop. If you run a home-service business and you can't find yourself on Google, this is for you."
57. "Delete your website. I'm serious — this version is losing you more than having none."
58. "Watch me break your website in 5 seconds... this is what your customers see on their phone."
59. "Screenshot this and text it to your web guy. Then ask him why it's not on your site."
60. "Pause the video and Google your own business right now. I'll wait. ...Not great, right?"
61. "This is the most expensive $0 you'll ever spend — the customers you lose with no website."
62. "Put your phone down and pick it up sideways. That's how 60% of people see your ugly site."
63. "I'm about to show you the 50-millisecond moment your customer decides you're legit or a joke."

## Pairing Hooks to CTAs (don't fumble the handoff)

A great hook wasted on a weak close leaks the lead. Always end with an on-platform action *before* any off-platform ask, and use a single dead-simple keyword so both the algorithm and your DM path stay clean:

- **Reach / teardown hooks →** "Comment **AUDIT** and I'll send you a free teardown of your site — 3 things costing you customers, no pitch."
- **Trade-callout / relevance hooks →** "Comment your **trade** and I'll tell you your #1 fix."
- **Proof / result hooks →** "DM me the word **AUDIT** and I'll do this for your business — free."
- **Reminder (US-wide):** comment-to-DM automation isn't native here, so tell viewers to **DM the word AUDIT** so ManyChat fires, and remember there are no clickable links in DMs — the teardown and booking link go out by email.

The hook earns the view. The free teardown earns the DM. The discovery call sells the wedge. Keep them in that order and never sell the build in the first three seconds.
